A receiver's movement is a fingerprint

We build each WR's fingerprint from a random half of his games, then ask: is the closest fingerprint from his other half his own? Out of 110 receivers it is 11.5% of the time, against 1.0% by chance. One route alone is too short to tell (about 2.8 s of tracking before the throw); a season of routes is not.

The release decides most of it. The features that most help the model are release speed, release accel and top speed, the same "get-off" and "long speed" that scouts grade.

Cooper Kupp → K.J. Osborn

The only budget match that is stable and built alike. Different routes, same motion: the same release speed and nearly the same top speed, at a fraction of the cost.

How it works

  1. Routes. Every WR route with PFF role "Pass Route", from the snap to the throw (or sack), flipped so all plays go left to right. QB scrambles and routes with pre-snap motion are dropped.
  2. Movement features per route. Release speed and burst in the first second, top and average speed, hardest braking, sharpest 1-second turn after the release, speed kept through that cut, path straightness, route time. Nothing about where he lines up.
  3. Fingerprint per WR. The average, 25th and 75th percentile of each feature, plus how often he makes a real cut (28 numbers), standardized. Receivers with 80+ routes (110).
  4. Is it real? Split-half test (above), plus a gradient-boosting model that guesses the receiver from one route, tested on games it never saw.
  5. Doppelgängers. Cosine similarity between fingerprints, teammates excluded, with a stability check across 20 random season splits.

Data: Big Data Bowl regional event data (NFL Next Gen Stats, PFF). Contracts and injury reports: nflverse. Code, notebook and every output: https://github.com/chirantan2003/shadow.

Limits

  • Most doppelgänger lists are not stable. A WR's top 5 overlaps only about 2× more than chance between season halves, because each half has only ~40 routes. Headline only the stable pairs.
  • Eight weeks, passing plays only, and tracking stops at the throw, so deep breaks after the throw are never seen.
  • Role, not just style. The teammates test controls for QB and scheme but not slot vs. outside roles. Several Giants receivers are among the hardest to identify, which may be a team or role effect we did not investigate.
  • Salary is approximate. We use the most recent contract signed in or before 2021, so fifth-year options are missed (Mike Williams shows $4.9M, not about $15.7M), and rookie deals make young stars look cheap.
  • "Moves like" is not "built like" or "produces like". Size and catch skills are not in the fingerprint.
